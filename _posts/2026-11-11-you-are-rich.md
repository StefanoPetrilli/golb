And let me be clear about it: I do not mind in a figuratively neither I mean it as a paraphrase.

I mean that, statistically speaking, you reading are a young cisgender white man in tech living in
a big city in the west of the world. And are also rich.

I was in India in October 2026 for an extended period of time it gave me the opportunity,
for the first time in my life, to start to contemplate what does it mean to live below the poverty
line. So far this is cliche but bear with me.

Living below the poverty line is not a prerogative of the east of the world or of developing
countries. Here in Europe there are people that are not doing economically well. Even in the
most developed part of the world there are people that cannot afford food or a house.

Often people that are economically challanged in the west are also difficult to notice. From the
outside someone that is not doing good financially might still look or dress exactly like any
average person.
In big cities, gentrification segregates cities by wealth and relegate those on the poorer part of the
spectrum in specific places. In rich countries this makes poor people so few and far apart that it
 gives us the luxury of looking the other way and ignoring the problem.

From my limited experience, this is not the case in certain part of the world.
Poverty is not just more extreme but is also endemic and in your face 24/7. You can still take the
blue pill and do a serious effort to try to ignore it. If you decide to take the red pill
instead, you have to embrace what you have in front of you and deal with the cognitive dissonance.

You have to admit that indeed you are rich, that you were born in the right part of the world
and that you have everything it takes to do anything you want.

Sure enough the west is no wonder land. Life here is not all roses and fllowers but if you had
seen what I have seen you would agree that we are pretty darn luky. And pretty darn rich.

If you compare yourself only with you neighbor, it's easy to trick yourself into tinking that you are
not rich, but, this is just a textbook example of (proximity bias)[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proximity_bias].

What you should be comparing yourself against is really the world population.

45% of the population lives with less than 8.30$/day per person and if you ended up reading this
I bet you are art of the demography that I mentioned at the beginning and it means that this might
be what you spend daily at for a sturbucks coffe.

Personally this realiaation offered me an interesting new way of framing things.

Accept that you are in the elite. When complaining about greedy rich people just remember
that you are one of them and ask yourself what are you doing to give back and share what you have.
