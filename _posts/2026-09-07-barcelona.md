# THREE-DAY BASIC ITINERARY

If I had only three days in Barcelona I would visit:

## Day 1: Ciutat Vella

This is the oldest and most historical part of the city. You can start the sighseeing from
[Plaza Catalunya](https://maps.app.goo.gl/FNy8pLboXpNP9JzA7) and go down the Rambla. To the East,
there is the gothic district and to the West the Raval.
This area of Barcelona has been settled for over 2000 years. If you pay close attention to your
environment while walking in this area you can notice how history is stratified in this area.

The city was founded by romans and was encircled by roman walls untill medieval times.
[You can see a map of where the roman walls used to be at this link](http://cartaarqueologica.bcn.cat/736).
Part of these walls are still standing to this day and are often mymetized with the environment.
The remainings are more or less visible, one of the best place to have a clear view at it is
[here](https://maps.app.goo.gl/myW76Wmx5ES2o2NXA) where a roman wall is now the base for a more
recent building and [here](https://maps.app.goo.gl/bnA9kYUvPEEL9tTg6) where roman columns have been
re discovered at the beginning of 1900 while renovating a medieval building.

Going forward in the time line you can see the [Catedral de Barcelona](https://maps.app.goo.gl/t5StUXeeL44gho238)
and the countless other medieval churchess and building in gotic style which give the name to the
district.

And finally, from modern times you can see the [Mercado de Santa Caterina](https://maps.app.goo.gl/g5y9c6AVBQLvV6Wd7),
[Plaza Reial](https://maps.app.goo.gl/hB8gWdyDXd6Lo7ka7) and [Palau Guell](https://maps.app.goo.gl/JEQT76NDDG4w7ESs5).

### Palm Trees in Plaza Reial

This is a niche trivia and a fun fact. Below the Plaza Reial there is not soil but the rubbles of
the building previously standing there. Because of this the palm trees standing in the square are
actually in huge planters rather than in soil.

The plants are constantly over growing their living space and need to be replaced. To avoid the
cost of replacing these palm trees, the city hall has a rent agreement with a provider which
takes care of swapping palm trees in the square as needed.

So essentially the palm tress in the square are rented and they get swapped as they overgrow the
planter where they are standing.

# TRANSPORT

The public transport in Barcelona is amazing! There are 12 lines of metro, 8 lines of sub-urban rails,
 6 lines of tram and countless busses and they are ever expanding it!

Because of how good the public transport is, how small the city is and the amount of traffic and
traffic lights, taking a car or a taxi in the city center is almost always a sub optimal choice.

## Biking

The optimal transport you have in Barcelona is by bike. You can get almost everywhere without ever
leaving a biking lane, the city is fairly flat unless you have to travel north-west.

The best bikes are the red one from the city hall. You will see them everywhere. Unfortunately, they
are reserved to the resident so as a tourist you can't rent them.
So you options are just renting from private companies.

## Which transport ticket to use

One standard trip on a bus or a metro in barcelona costs 2.90 euros. There are also pass for 24,
48 or more hours but they generally end up not being worth it. What I reccoment is the 10 trips
ticket which costs 12.15 euros.

## How to get from the airport to the city center

In Barcelona there are 4 options to go from the airport to the city center and it can get quite
configusing.

If you are not in a rush, the cheapest way to get back to the city is by using the bus 46. It runs
only between 5 AM and midnight. During the night you can use the N18 or the N17 and they do the same
route. This bus costs like a normal bus ticket or, if you have the 10 trips card, you it is counted
as one of the trips.

These busses bring you to Plaza Espanya which is fairly central and well connected by metro or bus
to any destination you might need to reach.

The other options do not count as a normal trip and are more expensive.

If you are in a rush, the best option is the Aereobus A1/A2 bus. It's a blue bus you can take from the
airport and stops in Plaza Espanya, in Gran Via and in Plaza Catalunya. This is the fastest option
and runs 24 hours. It costs 7.75 euros each trip.

You can also take the metro but it would almost always end up taking longer than the busses. To take
the metro from the airport you need to pay a supplement which brings the final cost to 5.90 euors.

Finally there is the train. Never took it. Probably only conveninet for specific locations.

In any case, consult maps.
