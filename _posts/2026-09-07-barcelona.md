# THREE-DAY BASIC ITINERARY

If I had only three days in Barcelona I would visit:

## Day 1: Ciutat Vella

This is the oldest and most historic part of the city. You can start sightseeing from
[Plaza Catalunya](https://maps.app.goo.gl/FNy8pLboXpNP9JzA7) and go down the Rambla. To the east
there is the Gothic district and to the west the Raval.
This area of Barcelona has been settled for over 2000 years. If you pay close attention to your
environment while walking in this area, you can notice how history is stratified.

The city was founded by the Romans and was encircled by Roman walls until medieval times.
[You can see a map of where the Roman walls used to be at this link](http://cartaarqueologica.bcn.cat/736).
Parts of these walls are still standing to this day and are often incorporated into more modern
buildings. The remains are more or less visible; one of the best places to get a clear view of them is
[here](https://maps.app.goo.gl/myW76Wmx5ES2o2NXA) where a Roman wall is now the base of a more
recent building, and [here](https://maps.app.goo.gl/bnA9kYUvPEEL9tTg6) where Roman columns were
rediscovered at the beginning of the 1900s while renovating a medieval building.

Going forward in the timeline, you can see the [Catedral de Barcelona](https://maps.app.goo.gl/t5StUXeeL44gho238)
and the countless other medieval churches and buildings in Gothic style which give the district its name.

And finally, from modern times, you can see the [Mercado de Santa Caterina](https://maps.app.goo.gl/g5y9c6AVBQLvV6Wd7),
[Plaza Reial](https://maps.app.goo.gl/hB8gWdyDXd6Lo7ka7) and [Palau Güell](https://maps.app.goo.gl/JEQT76NDDG4w7ESs5).

I recommend getting a guided tour of the district and then walking and exploring it at your own pace.

## Day 2: Gaudí and Gràcia

When talking about Barcelona, Gaudí is surely one of the first things that come to mind.

The buildings that everyone visits are [Palau Güell](https://maps.app.goo.gl/JEQT76NDDG4w7ESs5),
[Casa Batlló](https://maps.app.goo.gl/JCwJa3rxCUr6Rk9k8), [Casa Milà](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Fbz6Q45hTt4taHyU8)
and the [Sagrada Família](https://maps.app.goo.gl/3KNbnZqv2wZ5diPe7).

The Sagrada Família is definitely the most impressive of them all and a must-see in Barcelona.

From my POV, the buildings are nice but if you only look at them you will miss 75% of the beauty.
Gaudí was a madman and his works were totally revolutionary for the time he lived in, so all his
works also carry the story of how society reacted to them and how he challenged authority, which I
personally like more than the buildings themselves.
In addition, his work is full of symbolism and red herrings.

It is not possible to grasp this symbolism at first sight. For this reason, I recommend also
booking a guided tour of Gaudí's work.
Entering the Sagrada Família is worth it but not a must. If you decide to enter, you have to book
the tickets many weeks in advance.

Gràcia is a district of Barcelona that has been less touched by tourists and preserves its local
population more than other areas. You can take a stroll in the area. [Plaza del Sol](https://maps.app.goo.gl/SMU2z34uDcfM7VBr9)
is an amazing spot for a beer and tapas. There are second-hand and vintage shops in this area.

## Day 3: Plaza Espanya and Montjuïc

You can start this day at [Plaza Espanya](https://maps.app.goo.gl/eJLd7EHrzSMh9Hqh6) and go up Montjuïc,
passing by the [Montjuïc Magic Fountain](https://maps.app.goo.gl/MJ356HkWfNYBXUwa9),
the [National Museum of Catalan Art](https://maps.app.goo.gl/QeVTuFHJVHNWVCyp6) (which is also worth visiting inside, but nothing special)
and going up to [Montjuïc Castle](https://maps.app.goo.gl/V9cmEqJoSRtJYfrw9).

In this area you can also see the [Cactus Garden](https://maps.app.goo.gl/8iLk36cdEkSj98qK7).

This is all doable in half a day. Fill the rest of the day with anything that inspires you.

# MORE PLACES TO VISIT

### Palm Trees in Plaza Reial

This is a piece of niche trivia and a fun fact. Below Plaza Reial there is no soil but the rubble of
the buildings previously standing there. Because of this, the palm trees standing in the square are
actually in huge planters rather than in soil.

The plants are constantly overgrowing their living space and need to be replaced. To avoid the
cost of replacing these palm trees, the city hall has a rental agreement with a provider that
takes care of swapping palm trees in the square as needed.

So essentially, the palm trees in the square are rented and they get swapped as they overgrow the
planter where they are standing.

### More Gaudí

Besides what is mentioned in Day 2, Gaudí left his mark with several other creations that are scattered
in the city.

Among Gaudí's first works are the [Modernist Lampposts](https://maps.app.goo.gl/xmWecabJawSsqWTy5)
you can find in Plaza Reial and in other spots around the city. Most people don't know that these
lampposts are by Gaudí.

Another place worth visiting is [Park Güell](https://maps.app.goo.gl/d9vXAAVTVEd689mK9).
Tickets are always sold out, so book in advance.

Some other buildings by Gaudí: [Finca Güell](https://maps.app.goo.gl/nyRM4Seei59wByVa6),
[Casa Vicens](https://maps.app.goo.gl/3w77DSd9WrGaJKk87) and [Bellesguard](https://maps.app.goo.gl/nAokmvtztZQwdniN6).

### [Carrer Consell de Cent](https://maps.app.goo.gl/GeJWRRSnCVqmLn1Z9)

The city council is pursuing an effort to make the city less car-dependent and, as part of this
effort, they are gradually opening streets primarily to foot traffic and reducing the space allocated
to cars.

A beautiful example is Carrer Consell de Cent, which is a very long street that has been entirely
transformed.

It is highly recommended to take a walk on this street to see what a city could look like if we
removed cars.

A must, especially if you come from a car-dependent place.

### Camp Nou

Whether you are a football fan or could not care less, it is worth considering going to a
Barcelona match. Amazing energy.

### [Bogatell Beach](https://maps.app.goo.gl/X9RshSttdJCs8Hwp8)

If you come during the summer, or really in any season besides winter, Bogatell Beach is where
you want to go. It is not far from the city center but is usually far less crowded than other beaches
like Barceloneta; the crowd is usually better and there are amazing chiringuitos where you can have a
drink or eat something after the beach.

# TRANSPORT

The public transport in Barcelona is amazing! There are 12 lines of metro, 8 lines of suburban rails,
6 lines of tram and countless buses, and the network keeps expanding!

Because of how good the public transport is, how small the city is and the amount of traffic and
traffic lights, taking a car or a taxi in the city center is almost always a suboptimal choice.

## Biking

The optimal transport in Barcelona is by bike. You can get almost everywhere without ever
leaving a bike lane, and the city is fairly flat unless you have to travel north-west.

The best bikes are the red ones from the city hall. You will see them everywhere. Unfortunately, they
are reserved for residents, so as a tourist you can't rent them.
So your options are just renting from private companies.

## Which transport ticket to use

One standard trip on a bus or metro in Barcelona costs 2.90 euros. There are also passes for 24,
48 or more hours, but they generally end up not being worth it. What I recommend is the 10-trip
ticket, which costs 12.15 euros.

## How to get from the airport to the city center

In Barcelona there are 4 options to go from the airport to the city center, and it can get quite
confusing.

If you are not in a rush, the cheapest way to get back to the city is by using bus 46. It runs
only between 5 AM and midnight. During the night you can use the N18 or the N17 and they do the same
route. This bus costs the same as a normal bus ticket or, if you have the 10-trip card, it is counted
as one of the trips.

These buses bring you to Plaza Espanya, which is fairly central and well connected by metro or bus
to any destination you might need to reach.

The other options do not count as a normal trip and are more expensive.

If you are in a rush, the best option is the Aerobús A1/A2. It's a blue bus you can take from the
airport and it stops at Plaza Espanya, Gran Via and Plaza Catalunya. This is the fastest option
and it runs 24 hours. It costs 7.75 euros each trip.

You can also take the metro, but it would almost always end up taking longer than the buses. To take
the metro from the airport you need to pay a supplement, which brings the final cost to 5.90 euros.

Finally, there is the train. I have never taken it. It's probably only convenient for specific locations.

In any case, consult a map.
